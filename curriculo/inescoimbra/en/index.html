Inês Coimbra

Graphic Producer and Layout Designer

Inês Coimbra was eighteen years old when she entered a small layout studio in Rio de Janeiro, at a time when a book was still assembled by hand, page by page, with columns of text pasted onto cardboard before being photographed to become a photolithograph. Thirty years later, she continues doing essentially the same work—laying out pages, treating images, and preparing each file for the printing press—although almost none of the tools from that first job have survived. She was there when photolithography ceased to be chemical and when printing plates began to be engraved directly from the computer. She also witnessed the slow retirement of chromes, which for years passed through high-resolution scanners until digital cameras made that step unnecessary. Those who learned the craft through each of these changes tend to know, with unusual precision, what a page seen on screen will become when it arrives on paper.


Much of this training happened in prepress bureaus, which for a long time served as the bridge between publishers and printers. She worked first at Rainer Rio and then at Mergulhar, where she coordinated the image treatment department. When she left to open her own studio, she set up an office on Praia do Flamengo that at its peak had ten employees and through which passed a considerable portion of the city's editorial production. Books from Objetiva, Nova Fronteira, Sextante, Record, and Intrínseca were produced there, as were editions of Piauí and Revista de História da Biblioteca Nacional, along with catalogs and art books for MAM, Andréa Jakobsson, Leonel Kaz, and Editora Capivara. During this period, she also spent three years as production manager at Editora Senac Rio.


At Giz, where she is the principal layout designer, Inês works for a smaller group of partner clients: Piauí, MAM, Funbio, Manati, Eleva, and Urucum, which publishes limited-edition art books for collectors, as well as Intrínseca and Editora Permanência, whose books she designs in full. She is also responsible for the studio's most complex projects, such as Missal Quotidiano and S. – O Navio de Teseu, and for the image treatment of art books that pass through Giz.
